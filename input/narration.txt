Daily News for Wednesday, November 11, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Anthropic launched the roadshow for its initial public offering on Nasdaq, marketing shares to investors ahead of a possible listing later this month, as it seeks a valuation reportedly in the range of one point two five to one point seven five trillion dollars.

Quantum computing.

The United Kingdom unveiled a package worth about one hundred eighty nine million pounds to move quantum technology from research into deployment, funding quantum networking testbeds, a university commercialization consortium, and cross government procurement including defense spending.

Google DeepMind and Google Quantum AI researchers described a system in which large language model agents search for new quantum algorithms and use a formal proof assistant to machine check their correctness, reporting large efficiency gains over published benchmarks in a company preprint not yet peer reviewed.

Quantinuum, the Japanese research institute RIKEN, and SoftBank expanded a partnership to build hybrid quantum and AI supercomputing in Japan, following an upgrade to a trapped ion quantum system now integrated with RIKEN's GPU cluster and the Fugaku supercomputer.

United States stock market.

Wall Street fell after October consumer prices rose more than expected, with core inflation holding at zero point three percent for a third straight month. Federal Reserve officials including Christopher Waller and Lorie Logan said a rate increase in December is now possible, a sharp reversal from earlier bets on a cut.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

Industry Minister Karel Havlicek said the government is not currently considering new taxes on banks, insurers, energy firms, or telecom operators, pushing back after a weekend trial balloon, as the existing windfall tax on oil refinery profits heads to a key parliamentary vote on Friday.

The Czech Statistical Office confirmed October inflation at two point four percent year on year, with core inflation still elevated at three point one percent. The central bank warned prices could climb back above three percent by early next year because of planned changes to energy fees.

China.

China's October consumer prices rose zero point seven percent year on year while factory gate prices jumped five point seven percent, the fastest pace in years, highlighting a widening gap between rising production costs and weak consumer demand.
