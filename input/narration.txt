Daily News for Saturday, November 28, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Bloomberg and The Information reported that Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang's personal ties to a Shanghai contact helped enable a network of firms behind more than seven billion dollars in Nvidia chip sales into China, with restricted Blackwell processors traced to a Shanghai data center linked to the same contact. Nvidia says it is investigating.

OpenAI filed counterclaims against Apple in their trade secrets lawsuit, alleging Apple pressured suppliers to avoid OpenAI's hardware devices and used restrictive employment agreements to block employees from joining OpenAI.

Quantum computing.

The US Commerce Department finalized a thirty eight million dollar CHIPS Act research award for Diraq, a silicon spin qubit company, to build American fabrication and testing infrastructure for its CMOS compatible quantum processors.

Algorithmiq and IBM Quantum ran a forty qubit quantum walk algorithm on real protein interaction network data using IBM's Heron hardware, cutting circuit complexity about a hundredfold and demonstrating an early, noise sensitive proof of concept for gene prioritization in life sciences.

United States stock market.

US stocks rallied in Friday's shortened Black Friday session, with the Dow gaining about one point two percent, the S and P 500 up one point six percent and the Nasdaq rising two point three percent, capping the major indexes' best week in months after weeks of AI spending worries.

Anthropic shares jumped about eleven percent on Friday to close above their initial public offering price for the first time since a volatile debut week on the Nasdaq.

Chip and AI infrastructure stocks rebounded sharply, with Micron, SK Hynix and SanDisk each gaining more than seven percent, while South Korea's Kospi and China's semiconductor stock index posted some of their biggest one day gains on record.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

The Czech Chamber of Deputies gave final approval to a fifty percent windfall tax on refining margin gains, aimed almost entirely at Orlen Unipetrol's two Czech refineries, expected to raise about five and a half billion crowns to help offset rising fuel costs. The bill now goes to the Senate.

Slovakia's transport ministry said vehicles not already using the EU's electronic tolling system must pre register on board units starting December first, ahead of the country's new national road toll system taking effect January first, affecting an estimated hundred thousand vehicles.

Slovakia's Social Insurance Agency confirmed the mandatory employer contribution to the second pension pillar will stay at four percent of gross wages in twenty twenty seven, unchanged for the more than two million Slovaks enrolled.

China.

A new report found European companies operating in China prefer diversifying their supply chains across multiple countries rather than complying with the European Union's proposed Made in Europe local content rules, pushing back against Brussels' reshoring agenda.
