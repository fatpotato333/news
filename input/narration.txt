Daily News for Wednesday, November 25, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang held an all hands meeting one day after his reinstatement, telling staff he had hurt them, admitting he violated the company's code of conduct, and saying he is in therapy. He confirmed he will testify before the Senate Banking Committee next week, along with closed door hearings before House Intelligence and Foreign Affairs committees, as the underlying investigation into his personal ties continues.

A new independent benchmark called ShellBench, built from more than ten million real world agent interactions, found that leading AI agents struggle with everyday tasks. OpenAI's top model still failed nearly half the time across repeated runs, Meta's agent succeeded less than forty percent of the time, and nearly sixteen percent of all attempts violated a basic safety requirement, such as mishandling a bank authentication token.

Quantum computing.

Silicon spin qubit startup Diraq finalized up to thirty eight million dollars in CHIPS Act funding from the United States Department of Commerce, converting an earlier letter of intent into a definitive deal to build manufacturing and testing infrastructure for its quantum processor chips.

A consortium led by Harvard and QuEra, including Nvidia, Anthropic, and three American national laboratories, won a twelve million dollar Department of Energy award to develop AI designed, application specific quantum error correction for scientific computing workloads, work meant to inform QuEra's planned fault tolerant processor.

Researchers from Multiverse Computing and the University of Toronto replaced part of Alibaba's thirty billion parameter Qwen3 language model with a compact quantum circuit and tested it on IBM's hundred fifty six qubit processor. A same sized classical neural network still performed better in nearly every comparison, in an early, unreviewed result the researchers describe as a mixed outcome rather than a breakthrough.

United States stock market.

US stocks were mixed Tuesday as Monday's Nvidia driven rally faded. The Nasdaq fell one and a half percent and the S&P 500 fell half a percent to seven thousand two hundred one, its eighth loss in nine sessions, while the Dow rose almost one percent to fifty thousand on strength outside tech. Anthropic's stock dropped below its eighty three dollar initial public offering price, a week after its debut.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babiš announced the government will restore the public transport fare discount for students and seniors to seventy five percent, up from the current fifty percent, reversing the transport minister's earlier claim that the twenty twenty seven budget had no money for it. The discount takes effect in July and will be funded by reallocating money within the state budget; last year the state paid carriers more than five billion crowns to cover such discounts.

China.

China's anti corruption watchdog opened an investigation into Chen Jining, the Communist Party secretary of Shanghai and a sitting Politburo member, making him the fourth senior Politburo linked official purged in Xi Jinping's recent anti corruption drive. The Shanghai post has historically been a stepping stone to China's top leadership.
