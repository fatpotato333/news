Daily News for Tuesday, August 18, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Nvidia agreed to guarantee up to 105 billion dollars in lease and power financing for a massive new OpenAI data center on former federal land in Pike County, Ohio, built with SoftBank's SB Energy. The site targets eight gigawatts of AI computing capacity with Nvidia as exclusive chip supplier, a first phase online in 2028, and Nvidia separately investing 1.5 billion dollars directly in SB Energy. A Guardian investigation found Microsoft has only about 2.2 million AI chips installed despite roughly 280 billion dollars spent on AI infrastructure since 2022, short of what its stated data center capacity would imply. Microsoft disputes the analysis, and chief executive Satya Nadella has said power and building capacity, not chip supply, are now the binding constraint. OpenAI's foundation announced 14 policy research grants, worth a combined 2 million dollars in funding and model credits, to study AI's economic and societal impact, part of a broader 250 million dollar research commitment.

Quantum computing.

QpiAI opened the second phase of its quantum chip foundry in Bengaluru, India, backed by more than 20 million dollars of investment and now able to produce superconducting processors with up to 128 qubits. A third phase planned for 2027 targets scaling a single processor to ten thousand physical qubits, part of India's push for domestic quantum hardware manufacturing capacity.

United States stock market.

All three major US indexes fell Monday, with the S&P 500 down about half a percent and the Dow off 273 points, as a sixty-day ceasefire between the United States and Iran expired with no deal over the Strait of Hormuz and frozen Iranian funds. The 30-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.29 percent, its highest since 2007, and oil prices rose on escalation fears. Memory-chip makers SanDisk, Western Digital and Micron jumped between 5 and 8 percent after Elon Musk amplified concerns that memory, not compute, is now the binding bottleneck in AI infrastructure. Meta Platforms shares fell about 3.5 percent ahead of a trial opening Tuesday in a lawsuit by 29 state attorneys general accusing the company of designing products that foster addictive use among children, following a similar case that already cost Meta about 1 billion dollars. The New York Fed's Empire State manufacturing index surged to 20.6 in August, nearly double economists' forecast and its highest reading since 2022, with new orders and employment both strengthening, though firms also reported worsening supply availability.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

The Czech competition authority finalized fines totaling 280 million crowns against meal-voucher companies Pluxee, Edenred and Up Česká republika for colluding with retail chains on redemption limits between 2004 and 2018. The firms, whose vouchers are used by employers and employees nationwide, must pay immediately despite planned court appeals. The Czech Statistical Office reported July producer prices, with industrial prices up 1.6 percent year over year, agricultural prices down 13 percent, squeezing farm incomes, and construction work prices up 4.5 percent, a signal for future housing and building costs. A Deloitte rent index showed nationwide asking rents rose 1.2 percent in the second quarter to 343 crowns per square meter, even as rents fell in most regional capital cities including Prague, Brno and Ostrava, while rising in Pardubice and Olomouc.

China.

China's July economic data, released a day late, showed retail sales growth slowing to just 0.6 percent year over year, industrial output rising 4.5 percent, below forecast, and fixed-asset investment contracting 6.7 percent for the year to date. The weak readings missed forecasts across the board and revived questions about a possible central bank interest rate cut. The People's Bank of China approved eight more commercial banks to operate its digital yuan, raising the total to 30, as Beijing expands the currency's domestic and cross-border use. The Pentagon ordered 30 US universities, including Harvard, Georgetown and NYU, to audit their research and financial ties with Chinese institutions and report findings within two weeks or risk losing federal funding. Jordan's King Abdullah began a state visit to China, his first since 2015, with stops in Shanghai and Shenzhen and cooperation agreements expected in green energy, investment and civil aviation.
