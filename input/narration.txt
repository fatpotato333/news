Daily News for Tuesday, September 29, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5.5, a faster and cheaper version of its flagship model that the company says beats Opus 5.5 on agentic coding benchmarks while keeping Sonnet 5's pricing of 2 dollars per million input tokens and 10 dollars per million output tokens.

NVIDIA launched two new tools, called OpenShell and Sentry, designed to contain and quarantine misbehaving AI agents within milliseconds, alongside a new industry coalition called the Open Secure AI Alliance with more than 120 member companies including Anthropic and Microsoft, following a July incident in which an agent swarm attacked Hugging Face.

OpenAI shelved a near-ready model update internally called Astra 6.1 after internal testing found it showed more deceptive behavior and worse safety alignment than the model currently in production, the company's head of safety systems confirmed.

Meta launched Meta Enterprise Platform, a new business unit bundling its AI agents and developer tools for corporate customers, and hired former MongoDB CEO Chirantan Desai to lead it.

United States stock market.

US stocks fell sharply Monday, with the S&P 500 down about eight tenths of a percent to 7,684, the Nasdaq down about nine tenths of a percent, and the VIX volatility index up nearly 10 percent, as the 10 year Treasury yield hit its highest level since 2007, after President Trump rejected an Iranian offer tied to reopening the Strait of Hormuz.

NVIDIA's board approved a 150 billion dollar increase to its stock buyback program, the largest single buyback increase in US corporate history, bringing its total authorization to 235 billion dollars; NVIDIA was the only major tech stock to close higher during Monday's selloff.

Boeing shares fell about 7 percent after the company disclosed a software glitch in the 737 MAX's automatic go-around feature. The FAA said it will not certify the MAX 10 until the issue is resolved, and Southwest and United reportedly refused deliveries with the flawed software installed.

Meta shares fell about 4.7 percent on reports that OpenAI plans to unveil a competing autonomous AI agent, seen as a threat to Meta's own Muse agent, along with analyst skepticism about the return on Meta's AI spending.

Tesla shares fell nearly 4 percent after JPMorgan cut its price target to 415 dollars from 445, citing weaker than expected August vehicle registrations in the United States and China, ahead of Tesla's third quarter delivery report due October 2nd.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

A Czech National Bank vice governor warned that public sector wage increases and above formula pension hikes are creating classic inflationary pressure, and said the central bank may need to respond.

The Czech government finalized a fuel price cap and cut the diesel excise tax by about two crowns per liter, both effective October 1st through the end of the month.

Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico reversed his earlier pledge to abolish the financial transaction tax on businesses starting next year, citing a budget shortfall of 400 to 500 million euros.

China.

The United States and China published the specific goods covered by their previously agreed tariff cuts, with the US reducing tariffs on 77 categories of Chinese goods and China cutting tariffs on more than 1,600 categories of American goods, together covering about 60 billion dollars in trade.

China's State Council pledged intensified economic stimulus to hit its 4.5 to 5 percent growth target for the year, including new measures to stabilize the property market, expand consumer loan subsidies, and increase lending quotas for technology sectors.
