Daily News for Friday, October 30, 2026.

China.

The U.S. Federal Communications Commission voted to bar all Chinese test and certification labs from certifying electronics sold in the United States, citing national security concerns. The ban takes effect in December 2028, with existing device certifications unaffected until then.

The Chinese Communist Party's Fifth Plenum concluded in Beijing after four days, adopting a resolution on party discipline and elevating military anti-corruption chief Zhang Shengmin to the Politburo, with no successor to Xi Jinping signaled ahead of the 2027 Party Congress.

Chinese insurers Ping An and China Pacific Insurance reported third quarter net profit plunging 94 percent and 68 percent respectively, as a slide in Chinese stock markets hit their investment returns.

Quantum computing.

Finnish quantum company IQM entangled all 54 qubits of its cloud-accessible Emerald processor simultaneously for the first time, a milestone it credits to about a year of calibration work and roughly halved gate error rates. The processor is now available to cloud users.

United States stock market.

Amazon's cloud unit AWS grew 39 percent in the third quarter, its fastest pace in about five years, and operating income hit a record 13.9 percent margin. Shares still fell as much as 10 percent in after-hours trading as investors extended worries from Alphabet's results about returns on heavy AI spending.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

The European Commission approved the final payment of about 23 billion koruna to Czechia under the EU's post-pandemic recovery plan, closing out funding for affordable rental housing, a new cancer institute at Motol, university campuses, and public transport electrification.

The European Central Bank held its deposit rate at 2.5 percent, with some policymakers flagging energy-driven inflation risks that markets read as raising the odds of a rate increase in December, a move that would feed directly into Slovak mortgage and loan costs.

Slovakia's financial administration reminded consumers that a new European Union customs handling fee of 2 euros per item on parcels ordered from outside the EU, aimed at platforms like Temu, Shein and AliExpress, takes effect on November 1st, on top of an existing 3 euro duty on orders under 150 euros.
