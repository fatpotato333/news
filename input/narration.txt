Daily News for Friday, November 6, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Independent evaluator METR reported that Anthropic's AI models are now advancing on research and development tasks nearly twice as fast as they did over the prior year and a half. The group estimated that AI systems themselves are responsible for roughly eighty percent of that acceleration, though Anthropic restricted its access to key internal data.

OpenAI pledged a new transparency standard for its data centers, including ending nondisclosure agreements with local officials and publishing construction plans. The move follows a wave of ballot measures this week in which voters across the country pushed back against AI infrastructure projects in their communities.

Google said it will spend seventeen billion dollars on its first data center in Wyoming, continuing its infrastructure buildout even as political opposition to AI data centers grows nationwide.

Quantum computing.

IonQ posted record third quarter revenue of nearly one hundred seventy four million dollars, more than triple what it reported a year earlier, and raised its full year guidance, citing strong demand for its Tempo quantum computing systems.

Quantinuum raised its own revenue outlook after signing the largest cloud computing agreement in its history, a multiyear deal giving a major financial institution access to its software platform and future quantum hardware.

United States stock market.

DraftKings shares fell about twenty percent after the company reported a wider than expected quarterly loss and cut its full year profit guidance, citing heavy spending on its new prediction markets business.

Airbnb shares jumped about twelve percent after the company beat third quarter revenue and profit estimates, posted record bookings growth, and raised its full year outlook.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

The Czech National Bank raised its key interest rate a quarter point to four percent, effective today, its second increase this year. Governor Aleš Michl said the move does not mark the start of a new tightening cycle, though mortgage and savings rates may begin shifting in response.

China.

China's commerce ministry warned the European Union not to expect concessions from pressure tactics, after Brussels confirmed plans for a so called kill switch mechanism that could quickly cut Chinese companies off from the EU single market. The warning marks the latest strain on the trade truce the two sides reached last month over Chinese hybrid car exports.
