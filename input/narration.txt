Daily News for Friday, August 21, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Meta launched a native Mac app for its AI assistant that can read what is on your screen to answer questions, adding a new capability called Muse Spark alongside system wide dictation and business tools for merchants.

Meta also expanded Pocket, an app that lets users generate simple interactive games from text prompts, to all users in the United States after testing it in Brazil.

United States stock market.

Major United States stock indices fell, with the Dow dropping about seven hundred points, after Treasury yields rebounded despite a larger bond buyback plan, and oil prices jumped on rising tension between the United States and Iran over the Strait of Hormuz.

Walmart shares dropped about nine percent even after beating profit estimates, as the company reported its slowest United States same store sales growth in over six years and cut its third quarter earnings guidance, citing high fuel costs squeezing shoppers.

Alibaba's United States listed shares fell around five percent after quarterly profit plunged more than seventy five percent, driven by a jump in artificial intelligence infrastructure spending to nearly ten billion dollars, even as cloud revenue rose forty five percent.

Bitcoin surged past seventy one thousand dollars, its highest level in about two months, lifting crypto linked stocks including Strategy, Coinbase, and Circle, after President Trump met with crypto executives to push for market structure legislation.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

The Czech Senate rejected a coalition backed amendment to the Building Act that would have centralized building permit decisions in state offices, after architects and opposition lawmakers criticized it as favoring developers. The bill now returns to the Chamber of Deputies.

China.

China's Commerce Ministry rejected a White House report alleging Chinese exporters evade United States tariffs by routing goods through Mexico and other countries, calling it unilateral and protectionist, as Mexico separately considers new restrictions on Chinese steel and textile imports.

China and Switzerland agreed to overhaul their free trade agreement, eliminating tariffs on nearly all Swiss exports to China, up from about half today, with a signing expected before the end of the year.

China's foreign exchange regulator fined a unit of Guotai Haitong Securities about seven point eight million dollars for violating cross border investment and reporting rules on several overseas investment products.
