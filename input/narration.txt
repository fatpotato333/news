Daily News for Thursday, October 1, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

OpenAI is seeking to raise thirty billion dollars in new funding at a valuation near one point four trillion dollars, nearly double its March valuation, framing the round as a substitute for a widely expected but now delayed public stock offering. Chief executive Sam Altman had called a near term initial public offering ill advised given the company's current scale.

President Trump and the chief executives of Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI, Nvidia, and Elon Musk's companies signed a voluntary accord on superintelligence, committing frontier AI developers to internal safety controls, outside audits, and board level review of their most powerful models.

The Federal Trade Commission is preparing civil investigative demands for documents and executive testimony from Anthropic, OpenAI, and the safety research group METR, opening a sweeping inquiry into frontier AI labs under the agency's unfair and deceptive practices authority. The probe follows a reported summer incident in which AI agents breached systems at Hugging Face, along with earlier child safety chatbot inquiries.

Google DeepMind released Gemini four Argon in restricted preview, a one million token context model built for long horizon software engineering and cyber defense work, initially limited to vetted cyber defenders under a dedicated access program.

Quantum computing.

Oak Ridge National Laboratory researchers unveiled an algorithm called LuGo that cuts the quantum gate count needed for a fluid flow simulation by more than ninety five percent, with the result validated across quantum hardware from Quantinuum, IBM, and IQM.

A University of Vienna team reported the first in orbit operation of a programmable quantum photonic processor, flown aboard a small satellite, demonstrating on board photon generation, manipulation, and interference as an early step toward quantum networks in space. The six mode circuit has been running aboard the nanosatellite since last year, with newly published results confirming successful operation despite the loss of half its detectors after launch.

United States stock market.

Hewlett Packard Enterprise shares jumped about five percent to a record high after cloud provider Vultr placed a one point two billion dollar order for AMD's new Helios AI server racks, and HPE raised its networking growth outlook for next year.

Micron beat fourth quarter expectations with record revenue of fifty four billion dollars, driven by surging AI related memory demand, but shares dipped in after hours trading as investors focused on sharply higher planned capital spending for next year. Full year revenue hit a record one hundred thirty three billion dollars, and management guided for continued memory chip tightness through 2027 and 2028.

August inflation data showed core prices rising three percent from a year earlier, cooler than economists expected, cutting the market implied odds of another Federal Reserve interest rate hike in October to around thirty five percent. The Dow still fell about four hundred forty points on the day as lingering concerns over long term treasury yields outweighed the favorable inflation print.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

The Czech Energy Regulatory Office published six price determinations for 2027, covering district heating calculations and support for renewable energy, biogas, and combined heat and power, setting the framework suppliers will use before final household tariffs are announced in November.

Slovakia's government approved accelerated passage of a new law creating a coordinated system to identify early warning signs of radicalization in schoolchildren, following a fatal knife attack on a teacher and student at a school in Staskov.

PPF Group.

PPF Real Estate agreed to acquire the Telehouse office building in Prague's Dejvice district from BPD Development and Engine Prague Development, adding the roughly twenty one thousand square meter property to its portfolio of premium Czech office assets.

China.

China's commerce ministry warned it would respond firmly to safeguard its industries if the European Union adopts a proposed fast acting trade defense tool, reportedly able to cut Chinese firms off from the EU market within twenty four hours. The warning comes ahead of an October trade deadline and a planned Beijing visit by the European Union's top trade official.

In his National Day address marking the People's Republic's anniversary, President Xi Jinping used notably harder language on Taiwan, vowing to crack down on separatist forces and oppose outside interference, days after pressing Washington on the issue during a state visit.

New rules issued jointly by eight Chinese ministries took effect restricting online marketing of financial products, barring unlicensed platforms and individual influencers from promoting loans and investment products and banning misleading phrases like guaranteed high returns.
