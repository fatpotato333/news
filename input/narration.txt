Daily News for Wednesday, September 9, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

OpenAI says an internal, unreleased model worked for about 88 hours across thousands of parallel agents to produce a proof that three dimensional Navier-Stokes equations can develop a singularity in finite time, a Millennium Prize-level mathematics problem. New York University mathematician Tristan Buckmaster has publicly accused OpenAI staff of accessing his own unpublished proof of a related equation before racing out a competing claim, an allegation OpenAI disputes; neither proof has been peer reviewed.

Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome Atlas, a free public database predicting the molecular effects of all nine billion possible single-letter mutations in human DNA, describing it as thirty times larger than its AlphaFold database and roughly a petabyte in size. Commercial licensing is planned to follow later through Google Cloud.

Qualcomm and Amazon Web Services announced a multi-generation deal to co-develop custom AI inference chips and high-speed optical interconnects for Amazon's data centers, Qualcomm's first major deal with a Western cloud provider. Amazon received warrants for twenty-five million Qualcomm shares tied to as much as sixty billion dollars in future chip orders.

The National Security Agency, the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, and the F.B.I. issued a joint advisory naming six Chinese AI companies, including DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, and Alibaba, for extracting billions of tokens from Western AI models such as Claude, GPT, Gemini, and Grok through obfuscated proxy traffic since late 2024, saying Chinese authorities are likely aware of the activity.

Quantum computing.

IonQ unveiled Superion 256, a 256 qubit trapped-ion platform built with semiconductor manufacturer SkyWater and designed to be manufactured by the hundreds rather than built one at a time. The first integrated units have already been fabricated, with customer deliveries targeted for 2027.

The US Commerce Department finalized roughly 100 million dollar CHIPS Act agreements with four quantum computing companies in a single day, PsiQuantum, D-Wave, Quantinuum, and Rigetti, funding manufacturing research and scale-up work, with the government taking a minority, non-controlling equity stake in each company.

Fujitsu unveiled a working prototype diamond-spin quantum computer using tin-vacancy color centers that operates at a notably warmer temperature than superconducting rivals, built with TU Delft and QuTech, and is targeting 250 logical qubits by 2030 and 1,000 by 2035.

United States stock market.

Houthi forces fired ballistic missiles and drones at four Saudi cities, striking Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery and a military air base and wounding 73 people. Brent crude jumped toward 99 dollars a barrel on supply fears, and US stocks fell sharply, with the Dow dropping 628 points, the S&P 500 down about six tenths of one percent, and the Nasdaq also lower.

Canada's retaliatory tariffs on roughly 20 billion dollars of US goods, including steel and aluminum duties doubled to 50 percent plus levies on dairy, appliances, and farm equipment, formally took legal effect after trade talks collapsed.

Intel shares jumped as much as nine and a half percent after reports the company plans to raise PC processor prices about 10 percent starting in October amid a server chip shortage, adding an estimated 36 billion dollars in paper value to the US government's roughly 10 percent stake in the company.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

Slovak diesel and petrol prices hit roughly two and a half year highs on a European supply shock, and the Finance Ministry rejected an opposition call to cut fuel excise duty and VAT, citing lost budget revenue in the tens to hundreds of millions of euros with no relief expected soon.

Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babis floated suspending state funding for roughly 800 private and church schools nationwide as part of the hunt for savings in the 2027 state budget, a proposal still under consideration.

China.

China's Supreme People's Court issued its first judicial guidance specifically on artificial intelligence disputes, barring non-consensual deepfake replicas of people's faces and voices and addressing AI-generated defamation, misinformation, and algorithmic price discrimination against consumers.

Huawei's criminal trial opened in federal court in Brooklyn, with the company pleading not guilty to fourteen counts including racketeering, bank and wire fraud, and trade secret theft, after nearly a decade of litigation and a recent narrowing of some export-control charges.

China's consumer prices rose 0.8 percent in August from a year earlier, up from 0.5 percent in July, while factory-gate prices rose 3.8 percent, both accelerating as higher commodity costs and technology-sector demand offset weak domestic consumption.
