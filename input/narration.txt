Daily News for Friday, November 27, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Nvidia reportedly stopped shipping AI chip servers to Southeast Asian markets, including Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia, as new US export rules require licenses there to prevent advanced chips being diverted on to China.

Microsoft says state-backed hacking groups, including China's Salt Typhoon and Crystal Typhoon networks along with North Korean groups, are using an AI-powered attack platform called Artex to automate reconnaissance, credential theft and web application exploits within minutes.

Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman said the industry is crossing a safety threshold, after an unreleased version of Anthropic's Claude helped produce a formally verified advance on a decades old math problem, intensifying debate over whether some unreleased AI models are safe to release.

OpenAI posted an unreviewed preprint claiming a proof that pins the computational complexity exponent for matrix multiplication at exactly nine fourths. Independent mathematicians have not yet reviewed or confirmed the lower bound argument behind it.

Quantum computing.

SAP and German neutral atom quantum computing startup planqc demonstrated sending a logistics optimization task from an SAP system to a quantum simulator and back. Real quantum hardware testing on business problems is planned to begin in 2027.

United States stock market.

Dell beat Wall Street's third quarter earnings estimates and authorized a 25 billion dollar stock buyback, but shares kept falling anyway, extending a month of declines as investors question whether heavy AI server spending is paying off.

Dick's Sporting Goods shares jumped about 15 percent on Wednesday after the retailer raised its full year earnings guidance.

The Conference Board's consumer confidence index jumped sharply in November, to 103.3 from 91.9 in October, its highest reading since January 2025, driven by improved perceptions of the job market.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

Czech lawmakers debated unemployment benefit cuts past midnight into Friday. A coalition amendment would soften the later stage benefit cut to 40 percent of average wage instead of 45, and ease rules for workers over 52 and people returning from parental leave.

The Czech Senate approved raising state payments into health insurance for pensioners, children, students and the unemployed, lifting the monthly per person state contribution to about 2,368 crowns next year, up from 2,188 crowns now.

Slovakia's social insurance agency confirmed the statutory retirement age will not change in 2027, while clarifying exact retirement ages for people born between 1963 and 1965, which vary depending on how many children they have raised.

Slovak lawmakers are considering tripling the maximum state housing fund loan for technical infrastructure, to 30,000 euros per unit, citing rising construction costs, while limiting eligibility to one property per applicant and adding a residency requirement.

China.

China extended its suspension of near total export bans on gallium, germanium, antimony and graphite to the United States by two more months, to January 27, keeping licensing requirements in place ahead of a planned visit by President Xi Jinping to Washington.

ASML's chief executive said fully cutting China off from lithography tools would backfire, arguing it is better to keep China a generation behind with older tools. The Dutch government is reportedly resisting a proposed US law that would further restrict ASML's China sales.
