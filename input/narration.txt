Daily News for Thursday, August 13, 2026.

Quantum computing.

South Korea's government unveiled a new national strategy called Seven Seed, naming quantum computing a priority technology and targeting a domestically built, error corrected one hundred qubit processor by twenty twenty nine.

Yonsei University in South Korea will install IBM's Nighthawk quantum processor on site in November, becoming only the second institution in the world to run the chip, pairing it with a new platform for drug discovery and genetic disease research.

United States stock market.

Cisco reported fourth quarter revenue of seventeen point three billion dollars and adjusted earnings of one dollar twenty two cents a share, both above estimates, with AI infrastructure orders topping nine billion dollars for the year, but shares fell about four percent after hours on cautious guidance.

Micron shares surged about six and a half percent, pushing its market value back above one trillion dollars, after industry commentary said AI driven memory chip demand will outstrip supply through twenty twenty seven.

Lumentum reported fiscal fourth quarter revenue up one hundred nine percent to about one billion dollars and issued guidance well above estimates, citing surging demand for AI data center optical components, and shares jumped sharply.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babiš said the government will discuss drought driven farm losses, including possible operating loan support for livestock farmers facing feed shortages, at a cabinet meeting on August seventeenth.

China.

The European Union's new packaging waste regulation took effect across the bloc, closing a loophole that Chinese e commerce exporters had used to enter through more lenient member states and imposing stricter recycling and producer responsibility rules.

China's national science foundation expanded a research partnership with Iran to include rare earth exploration and processing, technology that Beijing otherwise restricts from export.

China's electric vehicle sales fell for a seventh straight month in July as purchase tax incentives faded, even as exports kept climbing toward roughly ten million vehicles for the year, led by BYD and Geely.

A survey by the US China Business Council found that American export controls are costing US companies billions of dollars in lost sales to Chinese competitors without achieving clear strategic gains, since most restricted items are already available from Chinese or other suppliers.
