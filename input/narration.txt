Daily News for Friday, November 20, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Apple is reportedly building its own autonomous AI agent capable of controlling apps, browsing the web, and writing code, according to new internal job listings uncovered by Bloomberg. The project is still early stage, with general availability not expected until late 2027.

Quantum computing.

Google's Willow quantum chip won a four qubit game 98 percent of the time, versus an 89 percent classical limit, in a peer reviewed study published in the journal Science demonstrating genuine quantum entanglement across up to 45 qubits. Researchers cautioned the advantage narrowed sharply when the experiment scaled to 105 qubits, where it depends on an unproven mathematical assumption.

Separately, researchers from the University of Wisconsin-Madison and the University of Arizona released an open source tool called QECLean that mathematically proved the exact error correction strength of IBM's 360 qubit chip, a property previously only estimated.

United States stock market.

US stocks fell sharply Thursday, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropping over 1,100 points to close below 50,000 for the first time since June, as investors took profits following Anthropic's stock market debut and doubts grew about near term payoffs from AI spending.

AMD shares fell about 8 percent after short seller Hunterbrook Capital alleged a Chinese server maker on the US trade blacklist kept receiving AMD chip firmware. AMD denied wrongdoing and said it complies with export law.

Federal Reserve officials split publicly on the size of a possible December rate hike. Governor Christopher Waller said the Fed may need to shock markets with a half point increase, while Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said a larger move is on the table, as October meeting minutes showed most officials favoring further hikes.

Anthropic's stock opened 34 percent above its 83 dollar public offering price on its Nasdaq debut Thursday, but profit taking pared the gain to about 17 percent by the close.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

Building materials maker Wienerberger will close its brick and roof tile plant in Stod, near Pilsen, by June 2027, cutting about 100 jobs, citing weak foreign demand for building materials.

The Czech Finance Ministry says it will not fine businesses for mistakes during January, the first month mandatory electronic sales records return, giving a grace period before enforcement begins in February 2027.

German shoemaker Gabor will cut around 380 jobs, roughly half its workforce, at its main Slovak factory in Banovce nad Bebravou by April 2027, citing weak orders from the German footwear market.

Starting January 2027, Slovak buyers under 35 can finance up to 90 percent of a first home's value, up from the current 80 percent limit, roughly halving the cash needed upfront on an average flat.

PPF Group.

PPF's new asset manager, Arvum, received Czech National Bank approval for its first two mutual funds, a bond fund and an equity fund, aimed mainly at clients of its Air Bank and PPF banka units.
