Daily News for Tuesday, October 20, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Microsoft has pulled out as anchor tenant of what was billed as Britain's largest AI data center, a project with developer Nscale in Essex, after the local power grid connection missed its 2026 timeline. Nscale had pledged two billion pounds for the site, part of Britain's 2025 AI strategy; Microsoft says it remains committed to its broader partnership with Nscale elsewhere.

AI chip and RISC-V startup Tenstorrent closed a one and a half billion dollar funding round, more than doubling its valuation to six point two billion dollars, with backers including ARK Invest, Fidelity, and Morgan Stanley, to fund mass production of its Galaxy AI server systems and expand its chip licensing business.

Quantum computing.

Quantinuum says it cut the logical error rate on its Helios trapped ion computer tenfold, down to about five errors per million operations, using a color code method where each added layer of correction cut remaining errors roughly twelvefold, a milestone it calls a major step toward its planned twenty twenty nine fault tolerant system.

Nvidia and Germany's Juelich Supercomputing Centre ran the largest simulation yet of a fault tolerant quantum processor, modeling one hundred four qubits under realistic noise using over eight thousand graphics processors. The method modeled needs ninety percent fewer qubits than earlier approaches, which had only reached fifty qubits under ideal, noiseless conditions.

University of Edinburgh researchers, working with Polytechnique Montreal, developed a classical algorithm that matches or beats some photonic quantum computers on a standard benchmark test, using only about five percent of the compute previously needed, adding to skepticism around photonic quantum advantage claims.

Japan's RIKEN institute demonstrated high fidelity parallel control of five silicon spin qubits on a single shared microwave line, fixing a key cross talk error source and keeping gate fidelities above ninety nine point nine percent even when all five qubits ran at once, in results published in Nature Physics.

United States stock market.

The Nasdaq rose about one percent Monday as AI and chip stocks rebounded, with the semiconductor index snapping its longest losing streak since two thousand eight, ahead of a heavy earnings week including Tesla, Netflix, Meta, and IBM. The Dow slipped slightly, dragged down by a decline in JPMorgan Chase shares.

Cleveland-Cliffs shares jumped over sixteen percent after the steelmaker beat its own third quarter guidance, reporting six hundred twelve million dollars in adjusted earnings, and issued twenty twenty seven guidance above four billion dollars while flagging a major debt reduction expected in the fourth quarter.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

The Czech government, unions, and employers agreed on new public sector pay tables for twenty twenty seven, raising salaries by up to nine thousand crowns a month at a cost of about thirty four billion crowns, with teachers, nurses, and doctors negotiated separately. A labor code amendment is still needed, with final rates going to government in December.

Slovakia's economy minister said households will pay roughly seventy nine euros per megawatt hour for electricity in twenty twenty seven under the government's deal with Slovenske elektrarne, though talks on further price concessions continue.

Volkswagen told managers worldwide not to plan wage increases through twenty thirty as part of a cost cutting plan affecting six hundred sixty thousand employees, with unions warning the freeze could cut real wages by more than ten percent by then at Volkswagen's Slovak plants in Bratislava and Martin.

China.

The United States ordered DeepSeek's AI products removed from all federal civilian computer systems and barred further government purchases of tools built on its models, citing national security risks; the order extends to third party tools built on DeepSeek's models, not just its own app.

The State Department sanctioned five China based entities, including a company founded by a convicted former Google engineer, for benefiting from the theft of Google's artificial intelligence and supercomputing trade secrets, following the engineer's conviction earlier this year.

China's embassy in London formally protested after British border officials questioned and searched the phones of Chinese trade delegates, tourists, and relatives' visitors under the country's counterterrorism and border security law, amid rising espionage tensions between the two countries.

A Chinese manufacturer unveiled a new high resolution transmission electron microscope, now in mass production, that it says can resolve details down to point one nanometers, with state media framing it as a challenge to incumbents Thermo Fisher, JEOL, and Hitachi in semiconductor and materials imaging.
