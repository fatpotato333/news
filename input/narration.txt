Daily News for Thursday, October 8, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Anthropic launched Claude Haiku 5.5, a smaller and faster model it calls the cheapest it has ever released, with an adjustable effort setting and pricing cut by roughly seventy-five percent compared to the previous Haiku model; the company says it beats OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna on several published benchmarks.

United States stock market.

Stocks pulled back from record highs as the ten-year Treasury yield jumped to five point three six percent, its highest since April 2002, following hawkish Federal Reserve minutes and rising oil prices tied to Middle East tensions. The Dow fell about two-thirds of one percent, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq each slipped roughly a fifth of one percent.

Bitcoin dropped more than three percent to about eighty-three thousand dollars, triggering nearly seven hundred million dollars in forced liquidations and dragging down crypto-linked stocks including Coinbase, Robinhood, and Strategy.

Levi Strauss beat third-quarter expectations, with revenue up four percent to one point six billion dollars, and raised its full-year profit guidance, though part of the gain came from a one-time tariff refund.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

The average Czech mortgage rate climbed to five point seven nine percent in October, a seventh straight monthly increase and the highest level since January 2024, adding roughly eighteen hundred crowns a month to payments on a typical loan since March.

In Slovakia, lawmakers from the Smer-SD party proposed an eight point four percent pay rise for state and public administration employees starting in July 2027, as part of ongoing debate over next year's budget.

China.

The European Union and China opened a two-day round of trade talks in Beijing described as a last chance to reach a deal on rare earths and market access before Brussels' self-imposed October deadline.

Separately, the European Parliament adopted a tougher stance on China covering trade dumping and espionage concerns, backing stronger anti-dumping duties and tighter screening of outbound investment into China.

The United States issued its first-ever fine under new outbound investment security rules, penalizing a venture firm two hundred thousand dollars for failing to report an investment in a Shanghai-based robotics and AI company.

Donald Trump reportedly asked Xi Jinping to ease China's export curbs on Japan, but Xi declined and repeated warnings about Japanese militarism, according to sources.
