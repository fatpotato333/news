Daily News for Wednesday, November 18, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Microsoft used its Ignite conference to launch an in-house coding model called MAI Code 2 and a new cybersecurity model, pitching both as lower-cost alternatives to OpenAI and Anthropic.

Anthropic's amended IPO filing shows Amazon and Google will together own nearly 30 percent of the company after listing, as a 26 billion dollar private placement from AMD, Broadcom, Microsoft, and Nvidia was also confirmed, with Nasdaq trading set to begin later this week.

Quantum computing.

Nvidia open-sourced a real-time quantum error correction decoding server for its CUDA-Q platform, hitting 157 kilohertz of throughput with under 6 milliseconds of latency; Quantum Machines plans to offer it to customers in December.

IQM announced that customers on its Halocene quantum system can now upgrade from 150 qubits toward more than 1,000 qubits without downtime, a step toward fault-tolerant machines unveiled at the SC26 conference.

United States stock market.

Nvidia shares jumped as much as 4.6 percent after hours as fiscal third-quarter revenue surged 103 percent to about 116 billion dollars, beating estimates, with next-quarter guidance raised to 130 billion dollars in the company's first earnings report since Jensen Huang's resignation.

Wall Street fell for a fourth straight session, with the Dow sinking about 955 points and the S&P 500 closing near a four-month low, as chip-stock jitters ahead of Nvidia's earnings combined with a reported internal network outage at the NSA to unsettle markets.

Salesforce shares plunged about 9.4 percent, their worst day since May of 2024, with no company news behind it; analysts blamed a software-sector selloff driven by fears that AI agents will erode traditional per-seat software pricing.

Markets now price a 96 to 98 percent chance the Federal Reserve raises rates a quarter point in December, after New York Fed President John Williams ruled out a bigger move but said inflation work is not done; October retail sales also topped forecasts.

Home Depot beat third-quarter estimates with sales up nearly 5 percent and comparable sales up 2.2 percent, but shares fell on cautious full-year guidance; the company also named Richard McPhail its next chief executive, effective November 23.

China.

Xi Jinping opened the APEC summit in Shenzhen, pledging tariff cuts and freer trade and calling for more international AI cooperation, setting the stage for his meeting with Donald Trump expected in the next day.
