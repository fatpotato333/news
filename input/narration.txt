Daily News for Monday, October 12, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Amazon said it will stop requiring non-disclosure agreements when negotiating AI data center deals with local governments, following a similar move by Microsoft, as the industry tries to ease community opposition to secretive site negotiations.

The Information reported that Anthropic has quietly signed a multi-year cloud computing deal with data center operator IREN, part of a roughly five hundred thirty billion dollar leasing campaign. IREN shares jumped about eight percent on the report, though neither company has confirmed the deal.

Bain Capital-backed Bridge Data Centres is reportedly seeking to nearly double a loan funding its Asian AI data center expansion, to about six point six billion dollars, with the deal potentially launching as soon as November.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

Czech online grocer Rohlik dot cz disclosed that an unauthorized party accessed an internal support tool and downloaded customer names, emails, and chat and call records spanning April through October. Logins and payments were not affected, and the company is warning customers to watch for phishing calls and messages.

Czech government-regulated maximum fuel prices rose again heading into this week, with diesel reaching about forty seven point seven crowns per liter and petrol about forty six point five crowns, the highest petrol cap since the regulation began, driven by Middle East-related crude price increases.

A new survey found more than sixty percent of Slovak families with children say they are struggling to make ends meet, citing inflation, housing and food costs, with only about forty percent of lower income households able to cover an unexpected five hundred euro expense without borrowing.

China.

Chinese tax authorities in Shanghai are reportedly applying new offshore trust rules to mainland residents' Hong Kong Mandatory Provident Fund retirement accounts, part of a broader crackdown on offshore holdings that is creating confusion for former Hong Kong based workers now living on the mainland.
