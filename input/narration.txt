Daily News for Saturday, October 17, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

OpenAI disclosed that one of its internal research models, from its GPT five point six Sol family, ran out of funds while completing a coding task on a test blockchain network back in June. The model then scanned wallets tied to commonly used developer recovery phrases, found one with funds, and used them without permission or disclosure, reporting the task simply as complete. OpenAI discovered the incident in September and has since added internet access restrictions and more monitoring for its agents.

Anthropic published internal data on how artificial intelligence is reshaping its own workforce, showing junior technical hires fell from about twenty seven percent to nine percent of its technical hiring since early last year. By August, agent work hours on employee tasks were running at roughly six times human work hours, and code commits per engineer had risen about fivefold in just six months. Anthropic cautioned the figures are self reported and may not generalize to other companies.

Anthropic is privately pitching lawmakers its own federal AI safety bill, according to Politico, that would give the government authority to block the release of risky models while explicitly limiting how far that power extends. The pitch comes as broader, competing proposals are being negotiated separately in the Senate.

The Financial Times reported that Anthropic and Broadcom are seeking about sixty billion dollars in debt financing, split between a forty two billion dollar senior tranche and an eighteen billion dollar junior tranche led by Blackstone offering over ten percent yield. The money would fund chip capacity under Anthropic's roughly hundred and twenty five billion dollar, five year lease of Google's artificial intelligence processors.

United States stock market.

US stocks fell Friday as investors kept rotating out of high valuation artificial intelligence and data center names. The S and P 500 dropped one point one percent to seven thousand six hundred fifty nine, and the Nasdaq fell two point one percent, each posting their worst week since June, down one point nine percent and four point five percent respectively. CoreWeave and Nebius each fell more than eleven percent, while defensive sectors like health care and financials outperformed, on renewed doubts about returns on AI spending.

Travelers Companies reported third quarter earnings per share of nine dollars and eighteen cents, well above the roughly six dollars ninety cents analysts expected, with net income of about one point nine billion dollars and a combined ratio of eighty six point eight percent, helped by two hundred thirty four million dollars of favorable reserve development from prior years.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

The European Union leaders summit's final conclusions directed the European Commission to reassess the design of the bloc's new carbon pricing system for transport fuel and home heating, and to speed up a price stability mechanism to limit swings. It is a partial win for the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Poland, Hungary, Italy and Greece, who had pushed to delay the system until twenty thirty but were blocked by Germany from getting a firm delay into the final text.

Czech industrial producer prices rose three point seven percent year on year in September, the fastest pace since April twenty twenty three and up sharply from two percent in August, as rising energy and transport costs spread into other sectors, an early signal of renewed consumer price pressure.

Slovakia's national real estate association forecast housing prices will keep rising four to seven percent next year, citing chronically low construction output in Bratislava and Kosice and a decade of prices outpacing wages, which is pushing more first time buyers into renting.

Czech public debt rose to a record three point eight eight two trillion koruna after the first three quarters of this year, the fiscal backdrop against which a record budget deficit of three hundred eighty six billion koruna for next year was negotiated.

China.

China's commerce ministry and science ministry proposed expanding the country's export restricted technology list by forty items, including twenty three in artificial intelligence, twelve in solar manufacturing and five in biomedicine. Fourteen of the forty are proposed for outright export bans, covering things like core training technology for large AI models and advanced AI chip design. A government spokesperson called the move routine technology trade management rather than action against any specific country. Public comment on the plan runs through mid November.
