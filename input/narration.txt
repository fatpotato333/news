Daily News for Thursday, August 20, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

OpenAI is testing a new privacy preserving safety system for enterprise and API customers that flags abuse patterns across related interactions without exposing prompt or response content to employees, aiming to keep its zero data retention guarantees intact. The move is a direct response to Anthropic's recent decision to require thirty day data retention for its most powerful models, and a full technical paper is due in September.

British chip startup Fractile is in talks to raise about six hundred million dollars at a six and a half billion dollar valuation, roughly six times its valuation from a raise just in May, after signing a quarter billion dollar inference chip supply deal with Anthropic. The company claims its memory compute fusion chips could cut AI inference costs by up to ninety percent, though the hardware is not expected to ship until twenty twenty seven.

Quantum computing.

IBM said it successfully joined and cooled two modular cryogenic cells into a single environment below fifteen millikelvin, validating the multi chip fridge architecture it needs to scale toward its Quantum Starling fault tolerant computer, targeted for twenty twenty nine. IBM plans to install its Nighthawk quantum processors into these modules later this year for further testing.

Cornell researchers developed a new process using krypton gas to deposit tantalum films for superconducting qubits at roughly half the usual temperature, while still achieving high quality factors. The lower temperature makes the tantalum qubits compatible with standard computer chip factory tools, according to a peer reviewed study published in Nature Materials.

United States stock market.

The Treasury said it will at least double its buyback operations for ten to thirty year government debt starting in September, a move that pulled long term bond yields down from multi decade highs and helped the Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq snap a three day losing streak.

Minutes from the Federal Reserve's late July meeting, released Wednesday, showed several officials favored raising interest rates and warned further hikes could still be needed if inflation persists, tempering hopes for a rate cut at the September meeting.

Target beat earnings estimates with quarterly revenue of about twenty six and a half billion dollars and comparable sales growth well above forecasts, but shares slipped after analysts noted nearly a billion dollars of the earnings beat came from one time tariff refunds rather than core operations.

Lowe's beat profit estimates but missed on revenue and cut its full year sales guidance to the bottom of its prior range, citing continued weakness in do it yourself home improvement spending even as professional and online sales held up.

Chip stocks including Intel, AMD and Broadcom fell four to five percent and SanDisk dropped about nine percent in a broad profit taking pullback ahead of Nvidia's earnings next week, even as Nvidia itself held roughly steady after reports that China eased import curbs on its H200 chips.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

The Czech Senate approved a law requiring a clear withdrawal button and banning dark patterns on websites and apps that sell financial services such as savings, insurance and pension products online or by phone, sending the bill to President Pavel for signature.

The Senate also sent the EET cash register bill back to the lower house with amendments exempting cashless card and bank transfer payments from registration duty and removing the tax exemption cap on employer paid health benefits; the finance minister warned the changes could cost the state up to fourteen billion crowns in revenue.

Czech authorities reminded the public that all five point one million data boxes, including one point four million held by private citizens, must switch to the new datovka dot gov dot cz domain by August twenty sixth, warning that browser saved passwords will not carry over automatically and users may be locked out.

China.

ByteDance and Tencent have each received roughly ten thousand Nvidia H200 chips in recent weeks as Beijing eased its restrictions on importing advanced foreign AI processors, though authorities are reportedly still pushing companies to keep most units staged offshore in Hong Kong to protect domestic chipmakers such as Huawei.

China's new network data security risk assessment rules took effect today, requiring companies classified as important data handlers to conduct annual security risk assessments and submit reports within twenty working days, with less frequent review required for general data handlers.
