Daily News for Monday, October 26, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Elon Musk said SpaceX has closed forty billion dollars in debt financing to buy Nvidia chips for its Colossus two data center, with delivery expected in the first half of 2027. He also warned that debt markets are tightening for AI cloud providers, predicting many of the more than two hundred smaller players will fail or consolidate.

Apollo Global Management's chief economist found no measurable profit or productivity gains from artificial intelligence among S and P five hundred companies outside the seven largest tech firms, arguing most businesses still can't convert cheaper AI tools into higher margins.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

Prime Minister Andrej Babiš confirmed the government will not withdraw its bill scrapping Czech Television and Czech Radio license fees, but is still negotiating how to replace the funding; a voluntary payment option is now planned for the bill's second reading.

S and P Global Ratings reaffirmed Slovakia's sovereign credit rating at A with a stable outlook, days after parliament approved a 2027 budget with a deficit of nearly five percent of GDP.

China.

The Chinese Communist Party opened its Fifth Plenum in Beijing, a four day session focused on party discipline and anti-corruption that's expected to formalize sweeping leadership changes ahead of next year's party congress.

China's Liaoning aircraft carrier group passed near the Philippines and into the Philippine Sea around the same time as the US carrier Theodore Roosevelt, which is operating there during a joint exercise with Japan, Australia, Canada and France; the Philippine Navy shadowed the Chinese ships.

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un made an unannounced visit to the Chinese border city of Dandong, accompanied by senior Chinese official Wang Huning, underscoring continuing economic ties between the two countries.
