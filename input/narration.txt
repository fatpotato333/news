Daily News for Saturday, October 31, 2026.

United States stock market.

Wall Street posted its worst day since June on Friday, with the S and P 500 falling one point eight percent, the Nasdaq dropping two point nine percent, and the Dow down one point one percent, as investors questioned whether massive AI infrastructure spending will pay off, dragging down Nvidia and other chip stocks.

Intel shares crashed nearly twenty nine percent, their worst single day since nineteen seventy four, after the company guided fourth quarter revenue and profit well below Wall Street estimates, with its finance chief warning the PC slowdown will continue into next year.

Reddit shares plunged just over twenty nine percent after the company reported weaker than expected daily active users and issued soft current quarter revenue guidance, compounded by investor concern over its plan to restrict forum access to logged in users.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

Czech utility CEZ announced it will raise electricity prices about three and a half percent and gas prices about six and a half percent for one point six million customers without fixed contracts starting January 2027, citing sharply higher wholesale energy costs.

Slovak Railways agreed to raise average wages eight point four percent and pay every employee a one time three hundred fifty euro bonus starting December 2026, under a new collective bargaining deal with unions.

Slovakia's Fiscal Council raised its 2026 public deficit estimate to four point four percent of GDP, about six point four billion euros, citing higher defense spending and underbudgeted municipal costs, signaling pressure for further budget measures.

China.

German President Frank Walter Steinmeier met Xi Jinping in Beijing, where Xi urged Germany to keep a pragmatic policy toward China, as Berlin faces a record trade deficit with Beijing and pressure from Brussels for tougher trade measures.

China's foreign ministry dismissed as a smear a New York Times report alleging a Chinese businesswoman linked to a major Nvidia chip reseller passed intelligence to Beijing, amid an ongoing United States and Singapore investigation into billions of dollars in diverted AI chips.
