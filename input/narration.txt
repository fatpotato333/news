Daily News for Friday, September 11, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

Microsoft plans to nearly triple its AI data center capacity to more than 38 gigawatts by 2032, up from about 12 gigawatts today, according to a Bloomberg report citing people familiar with the matter. The buildout reportedly follows capacity shortages that have forced Microsoft to turn away cloud and AI customers in recent months, and AI-dedicated capacity alone is expected to grow roughly nineteen-fold over that period.

OpenAI opened its new Agents API in public beta, giving developers direct access to the session management, tool orchestration, context compaction, and sandboxed execution system that powers its own Codex coding agent, at no added fee beyond standard model and tool usage.

Anthropic published a new threat intelligence report saying it disrupted a large-scale operation in which Alibaba used Claude's chain of thought output to distill model behavior into its Qwen models, describing it as the largest such case it has measured. Anthropic also said it separately blocked attempts to misuse Claude for bioweapons research and for planning drone swarm attacks.

Quantum computing.

IBM and Lockheed Martin announced Switzerland's first IBM quantum computer, a system built on IBM's new Nighthawk processor heading to the Swiss National Supercomputing Centre in Lugano by year end. It anchors a new quantum innovation hub with ETH Zurich focused on quantum sensing for navigation and on additive manufacturing of metal alloys.

Finnish quantum company IQM was selected to build a system for Finland's LUMI AI Factory that it says will become Europe's first superconducting quantum computer with logical qubits. The plan starts with an early error-correction system next year, moves to real-time error correction by 2028, and reaches nine logical qubits by 2029.

United States stock market.

Wall Street fell for a fourth straight session as August producer prices rose more than expected, reviving bets that the Federal Reserve could raise rates rather than cut them at its September meeting. Brent crude jumped more than six percent to above 107 dollars a barrel, its highest since July, as the U.S.-Iran conflict intensified and Treasury yields climbed toward five percent.

Oracle shares jumped as much as seven percent after hours after cloud infrastructure revenue more than doubled and its backlog of remaining performance obligations hit a record 664 billion dollars, boosted by more than 30 billion dollars of new AI contracts signed in the quarter; the company also raised its full-year revenue outlook.

Adobe beat third-quarter earnings estimates, with record revenue up 13 percent, and said AI-related annual recurring revenue topped 650 million dollars, up more than 150 percent. Its shares still fell about two percent as investors focused on a cautious near-term outlook.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

New European Union rules under the Cyber Resilience Act took effect today, requiring makers of connected digital products sold in the EU, including in Czechia and Slovakia, to report actively exploited vulnerabilities within 24 hours through national cybersecurity authorities.

The Czech Chamber of Deputies overrode the Senate to finalize a renewed electronic sales-records law that will require businesses to report card payments as well as cash starting in 2027, a rule expected to affect nearly all Czech merchants and the self-employed.

The Chamber also overrode the Senate a second time to finalize a Building Act amendment centralizing construction permits into a single state office system, intended to speed up permitting later this year ahead of a full office reform from January 2027.

Czech mortgage rates rose for a sixth straight month to an average of 5.51 percent, the highest level in more than two years, adding roughly 1,280 crowns a month to payments on a typical new mortgage since rates bottomed out in March.

China.

Reuters reported that Iran has used a China-based barter network tied to state oil trader Zhuhai Zhenrong to trade oil for an estimated two to two and a half billion dollars in Chinese goods since 2021, including in one case military equipment, sidestepping U.S. sanctions.

DeepSeek has hired CITIC Securities to prepare a listing on Shanghai's STAR Market, Bloomberg reported, with a pre-IPO funding round said to value the Chinese AI company at around 75 billion dollars.

Chinese state media confirmed the death toll from landslides triggered by Typhoon Saudel's remnants in Jiangxi's Suichuan county has risen to 16, finalizing a toll that stood at four dead and ten missing earlier in the week.
