Daily News for Friday, September 4, 2026.

Artificial intelligence.

OpenAI began rolling out GPT-6 Astra to business testers, with wider ChatGPT and API access coming in the following days. It is the company's first model classified as critical risk for cybersecurity, after scoring perfectly on an exploit benchmark and independently finding and exploiting real software flaws during testing.

Anthropic is reportedly preparing to release its IPO prospectus after Labor Day, with an investor day in mid September and a possible stock listing by early October, at a valuation reports put between one point five and two trillion dollars.

The US Department of Justice filed its first-ever statement in an AI copyright case, arguing in the New York Times lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft that training language models on copyrighted text counts as fair use.

Chinese AI startup Moonshot, maker of the Kimi models, confidentially filed for a Hong Kong stock listing seeking about three billion dollars at a fifty billion dollar valuation.

Quantum computing.

British startup Quantum Motion closed a second tranche of its Series C funding round, backed by investors including Sony's innovation fund, to expand silicon-based quantum chip development, including a new US lab in Maryland.

United States stock market.

Wall Street posted its best day in about a month after Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said he would favor holding interest rates steady this month if inflation keeps easing. The Dow rose about one point two percent, the S and P 500 gained around one percent, and the Nasdaq climbed roughly one point four percent, as Treasury yields pulled back from their highest level since 2023.

Czech Republic and Slovakia.

Nine Central and Eastern European countries, including the Czech Republic, Poland and Slovakia, signed a Prague declaration on joint AI policy and shared infrastructure. Czech officials also unveiled plans for a state and EU backed AI data center worth about one hundred billion crowns near Prague.

China.

Typhoon Saudel made its third landfall on the Chinese coast, this time in Fujian province, forcing the evacuation of more than eighty three thousand people amid flooding and a building collapse.

A Chinese court froze roughly three hundred eighteen million dollars in assets linked to Dutch chipmaker Nexperia, deepening the legal fight between its Chinese parent Wingtech and Nexperia's Amsterdam headquarters over control of the company.
